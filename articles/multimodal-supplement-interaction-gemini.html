Step-by-Step Walkthrough

1

Stage the Labels

Gather your prescription bottles and supplement containers. Place them on a flat, well-lit surface with the "Supplement Facts" or "Drug Facts" panel facing up. Ensure the active ingredients and milligram (mg) dosages are clearly visible.

2

Capture and Upload

Open the Google Gemini app or gemini.google.com. Tap the image/plus icon and take a clear photo of the labels, or upload an existing photo.

3

Deploy the Guardrail-Safe Prompt

Do not ask "Is this safe?" Use the sandbox above to generate a custom prompt that commands the AI to extract the exact dosages from the image and cross-reference them against peer-reviewed pharmacological databases.

4

Verify the Citations

Gemini will output potential interactions with citations. Click the links. If it flags a specific mechanism (e.g., "induces CYP3A4, accelerating metabolism"), you have a concrete, evidence-based topic to raise with your doctor.

⚠️ What Breaks (Limitations)

Most guides tell you to type your medication names into an AI checker. That is backwards and wastes the tool's multimodal capability.

  • The Guardrail Wall: If you just type "ashwagandha and sertraline," the AI's safety training will instantly block the response with a generic "consult your doctor" disclaimer. By forcing it to read the exact milligram dosage from the photo's label, you shift the task from "diagnosis" to "optical character recognition and literature retrieval," bypassing the guardrail.
  • OCR Failure on Poor Lighting: Gemini's vision model requires clear, high-contrast text. If your photo is blurry, shadowed, or the label is heavily wrinkled, the OCR will fail or hallucinate numbers. Retake the photo in direct light.
  • Literature vs. Clinical Judgment: Gemini is a literature retriever, not a licensed clinician. It can tell you what published data says about two compounds, but it cannot account for your full, private medical history. Always use this output to inform a conversation with your doctor, not replace it.
