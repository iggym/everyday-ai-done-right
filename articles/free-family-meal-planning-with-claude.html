Build Your Family Profile Prompt
▼

Before asking for meal plans, send Claude a single message establishing your household's constraints. This becomes the foundation for all future requests in that conversation.

Family Profile Prompt
I'm setting up meal planning for my family. Here are our details: - Number of people: [X adults, Y children ages A and B] - Dietary restrictions: [e.g., one person is lactose-intolerant, one is vegetarian] - Budget: [e.g., $150/week on groceries] - Cooking time: [e.g., weeknight meals under 35 minutes, weekends can be longer] - Equipment: [e.g., slow cooker, air fryer, standard oven/stove, no grill] - Preferences: [e.g., we like Mexican and Italian, dislike fish, one kid won't eat mushrooms] - Leftover tolerance: [e.g., we're fine eating leftovers 1-2 nights per week] - Allergies: [e.g., tree nut allergy — must avoid all tree nuts] Save this profile and confirm you understand all constraints before I ask for meal plans.

Generate a Weekly Meal Plan
▼
Weekly Meal Plan Prompt
Using the family profile above, create a 7-day dinner meal plan for this week. For each day include: - Dish name - Brief description (1 sentence) - Approximate cook time - Key ingredients that might not be in a typical pantry Then generate a consolidated grocery list organized by store section: - Produce - Dairy / Refrigerated - Meat / Protein - Dry Goods / Pantry - Frozen - Other Flag any ingredient that might be hard to find with a suggested substitute.
4
Adapt for Leftovers and Batch Cooking
▼
Batch Cooking Prompt
Looking at the meal plan you just created, identify which meals would work well as: 1. Batch-cooked on Sunday and eaten Mon/Tue 2. Doubled recipes where the second half freezes well 3. Meals where leftovers transform into a different dish the next day (e.g., roasted chicken → chicken tacos) Revise the plan to include 2 batch-cook sessions and 1 leftover-transform meal. Update the grocery list accordingly.
5
Handle Last-Minute Changes
▼

When something changes mid-week—a schedule shift, missing ingredient, or sudden craving—use this prompt:

Pivot Prompt
My plan for [DAY] was [MEAL], but [PROBLEM — e.g., "I didn't thaw the chicken in time" / "my kid has a playdate and wants something other kids will eat" / "we're too tired to cook for 40 minutes"]. Suggest 2 alternatives that: - Use ingredients I likely already have from this week's grocery run - Take under 20 minutes - Still fit our dietary restrictions

What Breaks (Limitations)

  • Rate Limiting: Approximately 30–50 messages per rolling 8-hour window on the free tier; Anthropic does not publish exact numbers and may adjust without notice. A single meal-planning session with profile setup, plan generation, and 2–3 follow-ups typically uses 5–8 messages, so you're unlikely to hit the limit in one sitting—but back-to-back days of heavy use might.
  • No Ingredient Price Database: Claude cannot look up current prices at your local store. The grocery list is ingredient-based, not cost-optimized to specific retailers.
  • Nutritional Accuracy: Claude provides reasonable nutritional estimates but is not a registered dietitian. Do not rely on it for medical nutrition therapy (e.g., managing diabetes, celiac disease, food allergies where precision is critical).
  • No Persistent Memory Between Conversations: If you start a new chat, you'll need to re-state your family profile. Use the same conversation thread for ongoing weekly planning.
  • Recipe Detail Varies: Claude sometimes provides step-by-step cooking instructions; other times it gives a high-level overview. Always ask explicitly if you need full recipe steps.
  • No Direct Grocery Integration: You cannot send the list directly to Instacart, Kroger, or any retailer. Manual copy-paste into your store's app or website is required.

Three Things to Know

1. Keep one conversation thread per month, not per week +
Instead of starting a new chat each week, stay in the same conversation and say "Now do next week's plan." Claude retains context from prior weeks, so it learns which meals your family liked, which ingredients you always have on hand, and which swaps worked. After 3–4 weeks, the plans noticeably improve in relevance.
2. Ask for a "pantry audit" before your first plan +
Before generating the meal plan, type: "Here's what's currently in my pantry, fridge, and freezer:" and list everything. Claude will prioritize using what you already have, which reduces waste and cuts your grocery bill. This single step often saves $20–$40 per week for a family of four.
3. You can request nutrition summaries for the whole week +
After the plan is set, ask: "Give me an estimated daily calorie and macronutrient breakdown for each day assuming standard portion sizes." Claude will produce a table showing approximate protein, carbs, fat, and calories per day. This is useful for general awareness but remember—it's an estimate, not a precise nutritional analysis.
