Step 1 of 4

Step 1: Upload Primary Learning Sources

Log in to notebooklm.google.com and create a new topic notebook. Upload up to 50 PDF files, text documents, or open-access textbook chapters.

Step 2: Submit Socratic Prompt

Paste the prompt below into the notebook chat bar to establish active recall testing grounded strictly in your files.
You are an expert academic tutor specializing in active recall and source-grounded learning. Analyze all uploaded sources in this notebook. Your objective: 1. Conduct a diagnostic active-recall session testing my comprehension of key concepts. 2. Ask me ONE targeted question at a time. Do not provide the answer upfront. 3. Wait for my response before evaluating my accuracy. 4. When evaluating my response, cite exact page numbers or section titles from the uploaded sources to back up your assessment. 5. If my answer is incorrect, provide a subtle hint pointing to the relevant section rather than giving the solution directly. Begin by stating the top 3 core themes covered across these sources, then ask the first diagnostic question.

Step 3: Respond Without Looking at Notes

Answer the diagnostic question directly in the chat bar. The system evaluates your retention against exact source citations.

Step 4: Generate Citation-Backed Study Sheet

Prompt NotebookLM to summarize your identified knowledge gaps into a single structured revision sheet with page-referenced inline citations.
⚠️ What Breaks & Failure Modes
  • Out-of-Scope Fallbacks: The system strictly ignores topics outside uploaded files. It will not fetch external web answers.
  • Unindexed Scanned PDFs: Image-only PDF scans without embedded OCR text layers cannot be indexed. Ensure text is selectable before uploading.
  • 50-Source Cap per Workspace: Exceeding 50 files requires creating a secondary notebook workspace.

Three Non-Obvious Strategies

1. Source-Grounded Citation Anchor +
Every statement generated links directly to exact source snippets in your files, removing hallucinated answers common in open web models.
2. Synthetic Socratic Questioning Loop +
Forcing the AI to query you one item at a time builds neural retention far better than passively reading pre-generated bullet point lists.
3. Multi-Document Cross-Synthesis +
Uploading lecture slides alongside textbooks enables the engine to link professor emphasis points directly to textbook definitions.
