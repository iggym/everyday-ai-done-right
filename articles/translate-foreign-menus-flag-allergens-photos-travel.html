Translate Foreign Menus and Flag Allergens From Photos While Traveling

Step-by-Step Walkthrough

Step 1 — Open Google AI Studio

Go to aistudio.google.com. Sign in with any Google account. No credit card is requested at any point during sign-up or usage of the free tier.

Step 2 — Select the Model

In the model dropdown on the right sidebar, select Gemini 2.0 Flash. This model handles image inputs and is fully available on the free tier with the documented 1,500 RPD quota.

Step 3 — Upload Your Menu Photo

Click the image upload button (🖼️) in the prompt area. Upload a clear, well-lit photo of the menu you want translated. Straight-on shots with good lighting produce the most reliable OCR results. Handwritten or decorative-text menus may need a cleaner photo.

Step 4 — Paste the Prompt

Copy the prompt template below and paste it into the prompt field, customizing the bracketed items for your dietary restrictions and the menu's language:

I am traveling and have these dietary restrictions: [e.g., peanut allergy, celiac/gluten-free, shellfish allergy, vegetarian].

This is a photo of a menu written in [e.g., Japanese, Thai, French]. For each dish listed:

1. Give the original name in the source language
2. Provide an English translation of the dish name
3. Describe what the dish likely contains (ingredients, preparation method)
4. Flag whether this dish is SAFE or UNSAFE for my dietary restrictions, and explain why
5. If borderline, explain what to ask the server to confirm

Format your response as a table with columns: Original Name | English Translation | Key Ingredients | Safety Status | Notes

Be conservative: if a dish commonly contains a restricted ingredient in this cuisine even if not explicitly listed, flag it as UNSAFE.

Step 5 — Read the Structured Table

Gemini returns a table with every dish on the menu. Each row shows the original name, English translation, likely ingredients, a SAFE or UNSAFE flag, and notes explaining the reasoning. Scan for the red flags first, then explore your safe options.

Step 6 — Follow Up for Modifications

If dishes are flagged UNSAFE, ask whether simple modifications could make them safe:

For the dishes marked UNSAFE, are there any modifications I could request (e.g., "without the sauce", "grilled instead of fried") that might make them safe? List each with the exact phrase to say in [local language] to make that request.

Step 7 — Save and Reference at the Restaurant

Screenshot the table or copy it to your notes app. Bring it up when ordering so you can point to safe dishes and ask modification questions in the local language if needed.

What Breaks

⚠ Known Limitations
  • Developer tool, not a consumer app — you're using a web console with a prompt field, not pointing your phone camera at a menu in real time.
  • Ingredient inference, not kitchen inspection — the model predicts ingredients from dish names and common recipes. A dish listed as "vegetable stir-fry" may share a wok with peanut oil.
  • OCR degrades on stylized text — cursive calligraphy, decorative fonts, and low-light or blurry photos reduce accuracy significantly.
  • Regional ingredient ambiguity — the same word can refer to different ingredients across regions (e.g., "peanut" vs. "groundnut"; "seitan" confusion on some Asian menus).
  • Requires internet — this will not work offline or in areas with poor connectivity.
  • 1,500 daily request cap is shared across all your Google AI Studio usage that day, not just menu translations.
  • Cross-contamination norms vary by country — the model may not account for local kitchen practices like shared fryers or shared prep surfaces.
  • Reference information, not medical advice — for life-threatening allergies, always confirm directly with restaurant staff. Do not rely solely on AI output.

Three Things Worth Knowing

Why not just use Google Translate's camera mode? +
Google Translate gives you literal word-by-word translation but has no concept of your dietary restrictions. It will translate "Pad Thai" as "Pad Thai" without telling you it typically contains peanuts, fish sauce, and gluten. Gemini reasons about ingredients and cross-references them against your specific restrictions.
Can I do this on my phone at the restaurant? +
Google AI Studio works in mobile browsers, but the interface is optimized for desktop. The most practical workflow is to photograph the menu at the restaurant, then run the translation when you're back at your hotel on a laptop. If you need real-time translation at the table, use the result from a previous run you saved to your notes.
How accurate is the allergen detection, really? +
For common dishes in well-documented cuisines, accuracy is high — Gemini correctly identifies that massaman curry contains peanuts, that tempura batter contains wheat, and that tom yum is often shellfish-based. It struggles with fusion dishes, highly regional preparations, and items where the allergen is an implicit cooking medium (e.g., peanut oil used without being listed). Treat SAFE results as "probably safe, but ask" and UNSAFE results as "definitely ask."
