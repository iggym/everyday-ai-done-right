Plan Family Chores and Weekly Routines Offline

Split household jobs fairly across the family with a local model that works from your own list, with no app accounts for the kids to sign up for.

Why this works

A chore chart works best when everyone agrees to it. You can draft the fair split on your own computer, then let the family argue over the result, which is the point.

Step by step

  1. List every recurring jobWrite each task with a rough time estimate: bins 5 min, dishes 15 min, hoovering 20 min, and so on.
  2. Add constraintsNote who is away on which days, sports practices, and any tasks someone dislikes or cannot do.
  3. Ask for a draft rotaUse the prompt below. Ask for two options, each with a weekly table, so the family can compare.
  4. Review as a familyRead both drafts at dinner. Swap tasks by hand; do not regenerate until you have agreed on the shape.
  5. Save and reuseKeep the chosen rota as chores.md and update it monthly.

Copy-paste prompt

Create two weekly chore rotas for a household with the members and tasks below. Keep total time per person within 20% of each other, respect the availability notes, and never give a task to someone listed as unable to do it. Output a table per option: day, task, person, minutes.

Check your result

  • Time per person is roughly balanced
  • No one is assigned a task they cannot do
  • Two options were compared before choosing

Pitfalls to avoid

  • Estimates are guesses. Adjust after two weeks of real use.
  • Keep rewards and allowances as a family decision; a model should not set them.
