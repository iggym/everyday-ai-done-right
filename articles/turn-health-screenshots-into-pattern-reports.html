Step 1 of 5

Collect your screenshots

Open the health apps you already have — Apple Health, Google Fit, Oura, MyFitnessPal, a mood tracker, a sleep app, whatever you actually use. Take screenshots of the daily views for the past 7 days.

Use daily views, not monthly overviews — the per-day numbers are larger on screen and far easier for the model to read from an image. You don't need every metric; grab whatever you naturally check.

Open Google AI Studio

Go to aistudio.google.com and sign in with a Google account. No credit card — the free tier activates on sign-in. You'll see a prompt interface.

The default model should be Gemini 2.0 Flash, which is the free-tier model you want. If a different model is selected, switch to Gemini 2.0 Flash in the model dropdown at the top.

Upload your screenshots

Click the image upload icon in the prompt area (or drag-and-drop your files). Upload all 7–10 screenshots in a single request — they'll appear as thumbnail previews.

The model processes all of them in one go thanks to its multimodal input and 1M-token context window. You do not need to send one screenshot per request.

Paste the prompt template and run

Copy the template below into the text area alongside your uploaded images, then click Run. The model reads each screenshot, extracts the visible metrics, builds a day-by-day table, and lists cross-metric patterns it observes in your data.

I'm sharing screenshots from my health and wellness apps covering the past week. For each screenshot: 1. Extract the key daily metrics visible (sleep hours, steps, calories, heart rate, mood rating, active minutes — whatever each screenshot shows) 2. Organize everything into a single day-by-day table with dates as rows and metrics as columns 3. List any patterns or correlations you notice across the week (e.g., "days with higher step counts tended to have longer sleep that night") 4. Flag any single day that's unusual compared to the weekly average for that metric This is personal data summarization, not medical advice. I want observations about patterns in my own data, not health recommendations or concerns.

Save what matters

Copy the output table into a note or spreadsheet. The real value is in the "patterns" and "unusual days" sections — that's the insight your individual apps can't surface because each one only sees its own slice of your data.

Gemini sees all your metrics at once, which is the entire point of this workflow.

What breaks

  • Safety filters block medical-advice phrasing. "What patterns do you observe?" works. "Should I be worried about this heart rate?" gets blocked. This is the correct boundary for a non-medical tool — work within it. The prompt template is already phrased to stay on the safe side.
  • Screenshot OCR is imperfect. Small text, graph lines, overlapping chart elements, and low-contrast numbers get misread. If a number in the output looks wrong, check the original screenshot. Don't make decisions based on a single extracted number you haven't verified.
  • 1,500 requests/day resets at midnight Pacific Time. Not a rolling 24-hour window — a fixed clock reset. Hit the limit at 11:30 PM PT and you're back in 30 minutes. Hit it at 9 AM PT and you wait 15 hours.
  • Your prompts and images go to Google. The free tier may use inputs to improve models. Check ai.google.dev/policies for the current data retention policy. Don't upload anything you wouldn't store in any cloud service.
  • No memory between sessions. Each prompt starts fresh. If you want to compare this week's patterns to last week's, paste both summaries into a single new prompt — the 1M-token context window handles it easily.

This isn't a bug — it's the correct boundary for a tool that isn't your doctor, and the prompt template is engineered to stay on the passing side.
3 Midnight PT reset is exploitable ▸ click
The 1,500-request quota resets at a fixed clock time (midnight Pacific), not a rolling 24-hour window from your last request. This means if you hit the limit at 11:30 PM PT, you get quota back in 30 minutes. Most API quotas roll from your last request, so this fixed-reset behavior is unusual and worth planning around.
